\begin{abstract}
Complex sequence models are often evaluated against weak baselines or on a single forecast horizon. This reproducible study compares a compact Transformer with persistence, seasonal-naive, ridge autoregression, and histogram gradient boosting on the WDC--SILSO Version 2.0 monthly sunspot series. The protocol freezes the scientific study input through March 2026, uses common target-date boundaries across horizons and context-length sensitivity tests, fits normalization on the training era only, repeats Transformer training across seeds, and reports moving-block bootstrap error-difference summaries, high-activity errors, and test-era robustness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research question}
Does a compact Transformer earn its complexity over strong simple and tabular baselines at 1-, 6-, and 12-month horizons, and are any advantages stable across random seeds, activity regimes, evaluation eras, and context lengths?

\section{Data}
The study uses the monthly mean total Sunspot Number Version 2.0 from WDC--SILSO, Royal Observatory of Belgium \cite{silso}. SILSO identifies the data under DOI 10.24414/qnza-ac80 and CC BY-NC 4.0. The research input is frozen through March 2026 and verified by a deterministic hash over the exact month/value sequence. Later source rows are excluded from this bundle rather than silently extending the evidence.

\section{Forecast design}
All models use chronological target splits. Validation and test target boundaries are defined on the raw monthly timeline and reused across every forecast horizon and context-length condition. Scaling statistics are fitted only through the final training target. Evaluation is rolling-origin direct forecasting with observed history: later test targets may use earlier realized observations in their context, but never future observations or the target itself.

\section{Baselines}
Persistence and 12-month seasonal-naive forecasts provide transparent references. Ridge autoregression is a linear high-dimensional baseline, while histogram gradient boosting provides a nonlinear non-neural baseline. Ridge and HGB settings are selected by MSE on the same chronological validation era used for Transformer checkpoint selection; HGB internal random early stopping is disabled.

\section{Transformer}
The compact encoder follows the self-attention architecture family introduced by Vaswani et al. \cite{vaswani2017attention}. It uses a 32-dimensional projection, four attention heads, one encoder layer, feed-forward width 64, and fixed sinusoidal positional encoding. The fixed positional encoding prevents context-length sensitivity from changing the number of trainable positional parameters. Training uses early stopping on the chronological validation era and seeds 13, 42, and 73.

\section{Evaluation}
RMSE and MAE are reported for each horizon. Transformer-versus-baseline MAE differences use a 12-month moving-block bootstrap against persistence, seasonal naive, ridge, and HGB as a descriptive serial-dependence-aware uncertainty summary. The same paired comparison is computed for every declared Transformer seed, with across-seed delta mean, standard deviation, and direction counts reported alongside the primary seed. Errors are also separated retrospectively by high solar activity and by early versus late halves of the common test era. The activity group uses the realized test outcome relative to a training-era threshold and is an error-analysis diagnostic, not a forecast-time input. Horizon-1 context sensitivity compares 60, 132, and 264 months using identical validation and test target dates and the same maximum Transformer epoch cap and early-stopping policy as the primary study.

\section{Validity and responsible-use boundary}
This is a statistical forecasting benchmark, not a physical solar model or an operational space-weather forecasting system. The time series is nonstationary and cyclic; a model advantage on this frozen historical test era does not establish future or cross-domain superiority. Bootstrap intervals are descriptive, and repeated seeds are not independent datasets.

\section{Reproducibility}
The executable study is \texttt{src/run\_experiment.py}. Generated evidence is written to \texttt{results/metrics.json}, \texttt{results/summary.md}, \texttt{results/figures/}, \texttt{paper/results.md}, and \texttt{paper/results.tex}.

\section{Calculation audit and reproduction scope}
The Transformer has lower mean error than the simpler baselines at the 1- and 6-month horizons under the recorded protocol. The 12-month result is less stable: its three-seed mean MAE is 24.206, versus 23.973 for histogram gradient boosting, despite seed 42 favoring the Transformer. The seed-42 intervals against that baseline also cross zero at 6 and 12 months. This qualifies any broad claim that the Transformer is consistently superior.

Negative paired MAE differences favor the Transformer. Twelve-month moving blocks retain some serial dependence. Later test predictions may use already observed test-era history; this is rolling-origin forecasting, not one fixed-origin recursive forecast.
